The bridge from The Secret Place to Gate Zero

How to Pray

You do not need the right words. You need the right Person, a door you shut behind you, and somewhere to begin.

“But thou, when thou prayest, enter into thy closet, and when thou hast shut thy door, pray to thy Father which is in secret; and thy Father which seeth in secret shall reward thee openly.”Matthew 6:6 · King James Version

Everyone arrives by a different road: a divorce, a child, a diagnosis, a question, a hunger for wisdom, a hole where love should be. This page meets you at your road. Take the sections in order the first time, then come back to the one you need.

First, who you are speaking to

He is love, and He is your Father

Prayer is not a technique for getting God’s attention. It is a conversation with the One who already has yours. The Bible does not begin with a rulebook. It begins with a Father who made a garden, and in Genesis 3 the first thing He says to a hiding human is not a sentence. It is a question.

“And the LORD God called unto Adam, and said unto him, Where art thou?”Genesis 3:9

God was not lost. Adam was. That question is the pattern of the whole book: God looking for us before we look for Him. Follow it to the last page and the Bible ends the same way, with a call going out in both directions.

“He which testifieth these things saith, Surely I come quickly. Amen. Even so, come, Lord Jesus.”Revelation 22:20

Between those two verses, one thing never changes. He loves, and He says so.

“He that loveth not knoweth not God; for God is love.”1 John 4:8

“The LORD hath appeared of old unto me, saying, Yea, I have loved thee with an everlasting love: therefore with lovingkindness have I drawn thee.”Jeremiah 31:3

“But God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us.”Romans 5:8

“We love him, because he first loved us.”1 John 4:19

Two Hebrew words carry this love. אַהֲבָה (ahavah) is the love itself, from the same root as the command in Deuteronomy 6:5, “And thou shalt love the LORD thy God with all thine heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy might.” חֶסֶד (chesed) is love that keeps its word, the covenant kind that stays when staying costs something. The King James renders it “lovingkindness” in Jeremiah 31:3. You are not loved because you prayed well. You pray because you are already loved. Read 1 John 4:19 again, slowly. The order matters.

If you are here because you have been searching for love, in a marriage that ended, in a person who left, in approval you never got, notice the order. Love is not the prize at the end of your performance. It is the ground you start on. The Father in Luke 15 does not wait at the gate with a list of terms.

“And he arose, and came to his father. But when he was yet a great way off, his father saw him, and had compassion, and ran, and fell on his neck, and kissed him.”Luke 15:20

He is also your provider, and Jesus puts the order of things plainly.

“But seek ye first the kingdom of God, and his righteousness; and all these things shall be added unto you.”Matthew 6:33

“But my God shall supply all your need according to his riches in glory by Christ Jesus.”Philippians 4:19

Read the order in Matthew 6:33 carefully. “Seek first” is not a bargain where you pay with devotion and He pays with provision. It is a reordering of what you are afraid of. Most of us pray with our needs in the front seat and God in the back. Jesus turns the car around. When the kingdom is first, the needs do not vanish, they are simply no longer steering. For the single mother who is the only provider, the widow, the woman holding a household together: a father of the fatherless, and a judge of the widows, is God in his holy habitation (Psalm 68:5). That is not poetry. That is a job description He has taken.

Step one

Salvation: the door that opens the rest

Everything on this page moves faster once this is settled, because prayer is a child speaking to a Father, and the Bible says there is one way a person becomes His child. It is not by being good enough, and it is not by knowing enough. It is a gift, and gifts are received.

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.”Ephesians 2:8-9

“For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life.”John 3:16

“That if thou shalt confess with thy mouth the Lord Jesus, and shalt believe in thine heart that God hath raised him from the dead, thou shalt be saved. For with the heart man believeth unto righteousness; and with the mouth confession is made unto salvation.”Romans 10:9-10

Notice two organs in Romans 10: the heart and the mouth. Believing is not only agreeing. It is trusting with the center of you, and then saying it out loud, which is why the first prayer matters. You do not have to say it well. The tax collector in Jesus’s story went home right with God on seven words.

“And the publican, standing afar off, would not lift up so much as his eyes unto heaven, but smote upon his breast, saying, God be merciful to me a sinner.”Luke 18:13

If you are ready, here are words you can make your own

Father, I come to You as I am. I believe that Jesus is Your Son, that He died for my sins, and that You raised Him from the dead. I turn from my way and I receive Your gift. Forgive me. Make me Yours. Teach me to know You. In Jesus’s name, amen.

These are not magic words. They are an honest door. If you prayed something like this just now, tell one person this week, because Romans 10:10 says the mouth is part of it. Then read the Gospel of John, one chapter a day. If you have already given your life to Jesus, you do not need to do this again. Go on to the next section, because there is a prayer you will keep praying for the rest of your life.

Step two, and every day after

Repentance: the prayer you keep coming back to

Salvation happens once. Repentance is how you stay close for the rest of your life. Two words carry it. In Hebrew it is שׁוּב (shuv), to turn, to return, the same word used when a person comes home. In Greek it is metanoia (μετάνοια), a change of mind, thinking differently enough that you walk differently. Neither word means self-punishment. Both mean a turn toward.

“Repent ye therefore, and be converted, that your sins may be blotted out, when the times of refreshing shall come from the presence of the Lord;”Acts 3:19

Look at what comes after the turning: times of refreshing from the presence of the Lord. Repentance is not the price of His presence. It is the road back into the room.

The book to read: Psalm 51

If you read one chapter on repentance, read Psalm 51. Its heading in the King James says David wrote it when Nathan the prophet came to him after he had gone in to Bathsheba (the story is in 2 Samuel 12). It is the prayer of a man who is not minimizing anything. He does not say “mistakes were made.” He asks for an inside job.

“Create in me a clean heart, O God; and renew a right spirit within me.”Psalm 51:10

The Hebrew behind “create” in that verse is the same verb used of God creating the heavens and the earth in Genesis 1:1. David is not asking for improvement. He is asking for a new creation, because he knows he cannot repair his own heart. That is the whole spirit of repentance: I cannot fix this, and You can.

“And rend your heart, and not your garments, and turn unto the LORD your God: for he is gracious and merciful, slow to anger, and of great kindness, and repenteth him of the evil.”Joel 2:13

In Joel’s day, people tore their clothes to show grief. God says the display is not what He wants. He wants the heart torn open toward Him, and He lists why that is safe: gracious, merciful, slow to anger, of great kindness. You are not turning toward a judge looking for a reason. You are turning toward a Father who has already shown you His face in Luke 15.

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”1 John 1:9

Four moves you can pray any day

Name it. Say what you did or what you are carrying, in plain words. Own it. No excuse, no blaming the person who hurt you first. Turn from it. Tell Him what you are leaving, and ask for the strength to leave it. Receive. Say out loud, “I am forgiven,” because 1 John 1:9 says He is faithful and just to do it. Then do not carry it back out of the room.

Conviction and condemnation sound different. Conviction says, “You did wrong. Come home.” Condemnation says, “You are wrong. Stay away.” Romans 8:1 settles which voice is His: “There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit.”

“It is of the LORD’S mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.”Lamentations 3:22-23

That is why repentance is continual and not heavy. Mercy is new every morning. You are not climbing back up a ladder each day, you are stepping into a morning that was made for you before you woke.

Step three

The pattern Jesus gave

The disciples watched Him pray and asked the one question that matters.

“And it came to pass, that, as he was praying in a certain place, when he ceased, one of his disciples said unto him, Lord, teach us to pray, as John also taught his disciples.”Luke 11:1

“After this manner therefore pray ye: Our Father which art in heaven, Hallowed be thy name. Thy kingdom come. Thy will be done in earth, as it is in heaven. Give us this day our daily bread. And forgive us our debts, as we forgive our debtors. And lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil: For thine is the kingdom, and the power, and the glory, for ever. Amen.”Matthew 6:9-13

He said “after this manner,” which means pattern, not script. Pray along with it, one line at a time, and put your own life into each line.

The lineWhat it doesPray it in your words
Our Father which art in heaven, Hallowed be thy nameYou begin with who He is, not what you need.“Father, You are ______ (name one thing He has been to you).”
Thy kingdom come, Thy will be doneYou put His agenda ahead of yours (Matthew 6:33).“I give You ______ today. Do it Your way.”
Give us this day our daily breadYou ask for today only, not next year.“I need ______ today.”
Forgive us our debts, as we forgive our debtorsRepentance and release in one breath.“I confess ______. I forgive ______.”
Lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evilYou ask for protection on the road ahead.“Keep me from ______. Deliver me from ______.”
For thine is the kingdom, and the power, and the gloryYou end where you began, with Him.“It is Yours. I trust You with it.”

“Confess your faults one to another, and pray one for another, that ye may be healed. The effectual fervent prayer of a righteous man availeth much.”James 5:16

Step four: find your door

What brought you here?

Every believer’s intimacy with God begins somewhere specific. For one woman it is wisdom. For another it is a marriage that ended, or a child she is raising alone, or a fear she cannot name, or a quiet suspicion that there has to be more. The Bible is sixty-six books, and each one speaks to a different door. Choose the one closest to where you are right now. You can come back and choose another.

Two kingdoms

In the world, not of it

As you begin to pray, you will feel a tension. You still live in your same house, job, and family, and something in you has started belonging somewhere else. That is not a malfunction. Jesus named it in His own prayer for His followers.

“I have given them thy word; and the world hath hated them, because they are not of the world, even as I am not of the world. I pray not that thou shouldest take them out of the world, but that thou shouldest keep them from the evil. They are not of the world, even as I am not of the world.”John 17:14-16

Look at what He did not ask. He did not ask to remove you from the world. You are sent into it. He asked that you be kept in it. Withdrawing from everyone is not holiness. Being in the room and not conformed to the room is.

“And be not conformed to this world: but be ye transformed by the renewing of your mind, that ye may prove what is that good, and acceptable, and perfect, will of God.”Romans 12:2

“Wherefore come out from among them, and be ye separate, saith the Lord, and touch not the unclean thing; and I will receive you,”2 Corinthians 6:17

The three places to look are John 17:14-16, Romans 12:2, and 2 Corinthians 6:17. Notice the verb in Romans: renewing. The world presses the shape of your thinking from outside. Prayer and the Word renew it from inside. The Greek word for world in John 17 is kosmos (κόσμος), the ordered system of life arranged without God. You can love the people in it and refuse the system.

“Trust in the LORD with all thine heart; and lean not unto thine own understanding. In all thy ways acknowledge him, and he shall direct thy paths.”Proverbs 3:5-6

When the world’s logic and God’s collide, this is the verse. The Hebrew for “lean” is שָׁעַן (sha’an), to rest your weight on something. Everyone leans on something. The question is whether it will hold.

What grows in the secret place

The fruit of the Spirit

“But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith, Meekness, temperance: against such there is no law.”Galatians 5:22-23

Notice the grammar. It says fruit, singular, with nine faces. You do not collect them one at a time by effort. You stay attached to the vine (John 15) and they grow. Intimacy with God is not a means to produce fruit. Fruit is the evidence that intimacy is real. Use this list as a mirror in prayer: which of these is thin in me right now, and what do I ask Him for?

LoveGreek agape (ἀγάπη). Ask: whom am I withholding from?
JoyNot mood. It survives loss. Ask: where did I stop expecting good?
PeaceHebrew שָׁלוֹם (shalom), wholeness. Ask: what am I carrying that is not mine?
LongsufferingPatience with people. Ask: whose slowness am I resenting?
GentlenessStrength under control. Ask: where was I harsh this week?
GoodnessLove that acts. Ask: who needs something from my hands?
FaithFaithfulness, the Hebrew אֱמוּנָה (emunah). Ask: where do I need to keep my word?
MeeknessPower that does not need to prove itself. Ask: what am I defending?
TemperanceSelf-control. Ask: what rules me that should not?

The end of the story

Why Revelation should make you hopeful, not numb

If the news has made you numb, you are not alone, and Jesus saw it coming.

“And because iniquity shall abound, the love of many shall wax cold.”Matthew 24:12

Desensitization is not a sign you are a bad believer. It is on the list. The answer is not more alarm. It is more warmth, and warmth is built in the secret place. Revelation opens with a blessing attached to simply reading it.

“Blessed is he that readeth, and they that hear the words of this prophecy, and keep those things which are written therein: for the time is at hand.”Revelation 1:3

The Greek title is Apokalypsis (ἀποκάλυψις), an unveiling. It was never meant to hide anything. It pulls the curtain back so you can see who wins, and Jesus tells you how to respond when the curtain moves.

“And when these things begin to come to pass, then look up, and lift up your heads; for your redemption draweth nigh.”Luke 21:28

Lift your head. That is the instruction. The last prayer in the Bible is not a prayer of fear. It is a prayer of longing: Even so, come, Lord Jesus. (Revelation 22:20.)

The seven churches: seven postures of a prayer life

Chapters 2 and 3 of Revelation are seven personal letters from Jesus to real congregations. Each ends with the same line, “He that hath an ear, let him hear what the Spirit saith unto the churches.” Read them as seven mirrors. You will find yourself in at least one.

Ephesus (Revelation 2:1-7). Busy, doctrinally sound, tired. “Nevertheless I have somewhat against thee, because thou hast left thy first love.” (2:4) If your prayer has become a duty, this is you. Ask for the first love back, the way it felt before you knew how to do it right.
Smyrna (2:8-11). Suffering. “Fear none of those things which thou shalt suffer… be thou faithful unto death, and I will give thee a crown of life.” (2:10) If you are in pain, this letter has no rebuke in it. Pray from the wound. He is not asking you to be okay.
Pergamos (2:12-17). Living where pressure is heavy, and slowly making room for what God does not endorse. “I have a few things against thee…” (2:14) Pray John 17: keep me in the world and from its evil.
Thyatira (2:18-29). Growing in love, service, faith, and patience, with something unhealthy allowed to stay. “I know thy works, and charity, and service, and faith, and thy patience, and thy works; and the last to be more than the first.” (2:19) Good fruit does not excuse a tolerated compromise. Ask Him to show you what you have been tolerating.
Sardis (3:1-6). A reputation for life. “I know thy works, that thou hast a name that thou livest, and art dead.” (3:1) Prayer that is performed for people and not offered to Him. Go back into the closet of Matthew 6:6.
Philadelphia (3:7-13). Little strength, and nothing rebuked. “Behold, I have set before thee an open door, and no man can shut it: for thou hast a little strength, and hast kept my word, and hast not denied my name.” (3:8) If you feel small, this is your letter. He sees the faithfulness nobody applauds.
Laodicea (3:14-22). Comfortable. “So then because thou art lukewarm, and neither cold nor hot, I will spue thee out of my mouth.” (3:16) The numbness of our age. And the verse that follows it is the most intimate line in the book:

“Behold, I stand at the door, and knock: if any man hear my voice, and open the door, I will come in to him, and will sup with him, and he with me.”Revelation 3:20

That verse is not first addressed to the lost. It is written to a church that thought it needed nothing. Jesus is outside, knocking, asking to eat with them. To sup is to share a meal at close quarters. That is the secret place, restated for the last days.

The ten virgins: oil you cannot borrow

Jesus told the story of ten young women waiting for a bridegroom (Matthew 25:1-13). Five were wise and five were foolish, and the difference is told in one sentence.

“They that were foolish took their lamps, and took no oil with them: But the wise took oil in their vessels with their lamps.”Matthew 25:3-4

Read what the story does not say. All ten had lamps. All ten believed the bridegroom was coming. All ten fell asleep: “While the bridegroom tarried, they all slumbered and slept.” The wise were not more awake. They were more prepared. When the cry came at midnight, the foolish said, “Give us of your oil; for our lamps are gone out,” and the wise answered, “Not so; lest there be not enough for us and you.”

Scripture does not tell us in that passage what the oil stands for. Many teachers read it as the Holy Spirit, and others as a life actually lived with God. What the story makes plain is this: it is the one thing you cannot receive secondhand at the last minute. No one can borrow your intimacy with God. Your mother’s faith, your pastor’s sermon, an app, a great playlist: none of these fill your own vessel. Oil is stored privately, over time, in the dark, which is exactly what the secret place is.

Then comes the line that should stop every one of us.

“Afterward came also the other virgins, saying, Lord, Lord, open to us. But he answered and said, Verily I say unto you, I know you not.”Matthew 25:11-12

Not “you were not good enough.” Not “you were not busy enough.” I know you not. The question at the end of everything is about being known. That is what a secret place is for. And the instruction that closes the story is not fear.

“Watch therefore, for ye know neither the day nor the hour wherein the Son of man cometh.”Matthew 25:13

Put the two together. Revelation 3:20 says He is knocking now. Matthew 25 says there is a day the door is shut. So today is the day to open it, and to keep your lamp supplied: ten quiet minutes, a chapter, a confession, a thank-you. The excitement of the end times is not in knowing the schedule. It is in being ready, and loved, and known when He walks in.

Where it all lives

How to find anything in your King James Bible

If you have never opened one, the Bible can feel like a locked building. It is a library of sixty-six books: thirty-nine in the Old Testament and twenty-seven in the New. Every reference has three parts. The book, then the chapter, then the verse. John 3:16 means the book of John, chapter 3, verse 16. The table of contents at the front gives each book’s page, and a digital Bible lets you type the name. Start with the books below.

SectionBooksWhat it is for
Law (Old Testament)Genesis to DeuteronomyBeginnings, covenant, holiness. Genesis is בְּרֵאשִׁית (Bereshit), “in the beginning.” Leviticus is וַיִּקְרָא (Vayikra), “and He called.”
HistoryJoshua to EstherIsrael’s story, including Ruth, 1 Samuel 1 (Hannah), and Nehemiah.
Poetry and wisdomJob to Song of SolomonPsalms תְּהִלִּים (Tehillim, “praises”), Proverbs מִשְׁלֵי (Mishlei), Ecclesiastes קֹהֶלֶת (Kohelet, the Preacher), Song of Solomon שִׁיר הַשִּׁירִים (Shir HaShirim, “Song of Songs”).
Major prophetsIsaiah to DanielIsaiah יְשַׁעְיָהוּ (Yeshayahu, “the LORD saves”), Jeremiah, Lamentations אֵיכָה (Eikha, “How!”), Ezekiel, Daniel.
Minor prophetsHosea to MalachiTwelve shorter books, including Joel (repentance) and Jonah.
Gospels (New Testament)Matthew, Mark, Luke, JohnThe life of Jesus. Start with John.
HistoryActsThe first church, the Holy Spirit poured out.
Letters of PaulRomans to PhilemonDoctrine and daily life. Romans (salvation), Philippians (peace), Galatians (the fruit of the Spirit).
General lettersHebrews to JudeHebrews, James (wisdom), 1 Peter (holiness), 1 John (love).
ProphecyRevelationThe unveiling. The last book.

Rule of thumb: Psalms and Proverbs sit in the middle of the Old Testament. The New Testament begins at Matthew, book 40. The Bible has 1,189 chapters, and Psalm 119 is the longest.
