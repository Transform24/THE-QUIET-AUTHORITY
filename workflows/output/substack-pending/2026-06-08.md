# The Worm That Wore Crimson

Psalm 22:6
"But I am a worm, and no man; a reproach of men, and despised of the people."

The crimson worm of ancient Israel had one purpose: to give her life for her young. She would climb the wooden beam, attach herself there, and pour out a red dye so deep it could never be washed away. When she was spent—when there was nothing left—her body hardened into a waxy shell that sheltered her children for three days. Then she fell away, and what remained was white as snow.

Christ spoke these words from the cross, calling Himself this worm. He chose the image deliberately. He made Himself nothing so that you could be clothed in crimson, covered, redeemed. You have poured yourself out on wooden beams of your own making—the debt, the sleepless nights, the endless giving from an empty place. You have reproached yourself more than any man could. But you were never meant to be your own redeemer.

He already climbed that beam. He already poured out what could not be replenished. He already became nothing so you could stop trying to become everything. The shell you are living in—this hardened, protective exhaustion—was never meant to be permanent. He is inviting you to fall away from it, to let Him be what He already is: the One who dyes you clean, the One who makes you white as snow.

You do not have to save yourself. That work is finished.
