The Crimson Worm
When becoming requires everything you thought you were

Dear one,

I am writing to you in the stillness of this Sunday morning, thinking of you. Thinking of the woman who has given so much there is nothing left to give. The woman who lies awake calculating numbers that will not add up. The woman whose prayers have become whispers in the dark: "I cannot do this anymore."

The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he hath anointed me to preach the gospel to the poor; he hath sent me to heal the brokenhearted, to preach deliverance to the captives, and recovering of sight to the blind, to set at liberty them that are bruised.

This is why I write. Not to fix you, but to tell you what I have seen.

There is a mystery hidden in Psalm 22, verse 6. The psalm of Christ's crucifixion. In the middle of that anguish, He says something strange: "But I am a worm, and no man; a reproach of men, and despised of the people."

The word used there is not just any worm. It is tola—the crimson worm. In ancient times, when the female crimson worm was ready to give birth, she would attach herself to a tree. She would fix herself there and lay her eggs beneath her body. Then, to protect her young and give them life, she would secrete a crimson fluid—a scarlet dye—that would cover them. And in that process, she would die. After three days, the stain she left behind would turn white as snow.

This is what Christ did. He made Himself nothing. He became the worm, crushed against the wood, pouring out His life so that you could be covered. So that your scarlet could become white. So that in your death to yourself, you could find the life you were always meant to live.

You have been that worm, beloved. You have fixed yourself to everything you thought would save you—the marriage, the ministry, the bank account, the reputation, the role of the one who holds it all together. You have poured yourself out until there was nothing left. And now you are pressed against the hard wood of circumstances you cannot change, wondering if this is the end.

But what if this is not your destruction? What if this is your deliverance?

Christ did not become the crimson worm to show you how to save yourself. He became it to show you that death is the doorway. That surrender is not failure. That becoming nothing in your own strength is the only way to be filled with His.

"Trust in the Lord with all thine heart; and lean not unto thine own understanding. In all thy ways acknowledge him, and he shall direct thy paths." This is Proverbs 3:5-6, and it is not a suggestion. It is the way through. Not around. Through.

The debt that keeps you awake—He sees it. The shame of needing help—He carried it to the tree. The terror that you will lose everything—He already lost everything so you could be found.

You do not need another strategy. You need the Redeemer.

Consider these questions in the quiet:

What would it mean to release your grip on the one thing you are still trying to control?

Can you trust that the crimson stain of where you are right now might be the very place Christ meets you?

What if your emptiness is not evidence of failure, but an invitation to be filled with something you could never produce on your own?

Beloved, I bless you this Sunday with the courage to let go. With the grace to believe that God is not surprised by your crisis—He is present in it. With the truth that you were never meant to be your own savior. Christ already came. The work is finished. The crimson has turned to white.

You are held. You are seen. You are loved.
