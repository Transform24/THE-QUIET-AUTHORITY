The Hebrew Behind the Names

Original content, written from the Hebrew text and standard, publicly available root meanings. What each name is actually built from, before it's ever offered as a card to keep.

How to read this guide. Each entry has four parts: the Hebrew script, how to say it, what the word is literally built from, and the verse it comes from. No guessing, no folk-etymology. Where a name is genuinely debated among Hebrew scholars, that's said plainly instead of picked for you.
1
אֶהְיֶה
EHYEH — "eh-YEH"

Built from the Hebrew verb "to be." God's own answer when Moses asked for a name. Not a title borrowed from somewhere else. Exodus 3:14.

2
אֵל קַנָּא
EL QANNA — "el kah-NAH"

"El" is the general Hebrew word for God or a mighty one; "qanna" is the same root used for jealousy or zeal, the emotion of a covenant partner, not envy. Exodus 34:14.

3
אֲדֹנָי
ADONAI — "ah-doh-NIGH"

From "adon," master or lord. Used throughout Scripture in place of God's covenant name when that name wasn't to be spoken aloud.

4
עַתִּיק יוֹמִין
ATTIQ YOMIN — "ah-TEEK yo-MEEN"

Aramaic, not Hebrew (Daniel's vision is written in Aramaic at this point in the text). Literally "ancient of days," a being who exists outside of time's progression. Daniel 7:9.

5
אֵל עֶלְיוֹן
EL ELYON — "el el-YOHN"

"Elyon" comes from a root meaning to go up or ascend, God positioned above every other power. Genesis 14:18-20.

6
אֵל גִּבּוֹר
EL GIBBOR — "el gih-BOR"

"Gibbor" is the same word used for a mighty warrior or hero elsewhere in Scripture. Isaiah 9:6.

7
אֵל עוֹלָם
EL OLAM — "el oh-LAHM"

"Olam" means a long, unbounded duration, often translated "everlasting," though the Hebrew idea is less about infinity and more about a span with no visible edge. Genesis 21:33.

8
אֵל דֵּעוֹת
EL DEOT — "el deh-OTE"

"Deot" is the plural of "knowledge," a God of all knowledge, not partial knowledge. 1 Samuel 2:3.

9
אֵל רֳאִי
EL ROI — "el ro-EE"

From "ra'ah," to see. Hagar's own name for God after being seen in the wilderness when she believed no one was watching. Genesis 16:13.

10
יְהוָה צְבָאוֹת
YAHWEH SABAOTH — "yah-WEH tsuh-vah-OTE"

"Sabaoth" means armies or hosts, both the armies of Israel and the heavenly host. 1 Samuel 1:3.

11
יְהוָה שָׁמָּה
YAHWEH SHAMMAH — "yah-WEH SHAH-mah"

Literally "the LORD is there," the final line of Ezekiel's vision of the restored city. Ezekiel 48:35.

12
אֱלֹהִים
ELOHIM — "el-oh-HEEM"

Grammatically plural in form but paired with singular verbs throughout Genesis 1, a longstanding point of Hebrew grammar (a "plural of majesty," not multiple gods). The first name used for God in Scripture. Genesis 1:1.

13
אֵל מְחֹלְלֶךָ
EL M'CHOLELECHA — "el meh-kho-leh-LEH-kha"

From a root meaning to writhe, twist, or bring forth in labor, the image of God personally forming Israel the way a mother labors to bring forth a child. Deuteronomy 32:18.

14
אַבָּא
ABBA — "AH-bah"

Aramaic, not Hebrew, the informal, familiar word for father, closer to how a small child would address a parent. Romans 8:15.

15
אֵל שַׁדַּי
EL SHADDAI — "el shah-DIGH"

The root of "Shaddai" is genuinely debated among Hebrew scholars, commonly linked either to a word for mountain (all-powerful like an immovable height) or a root tied to sufficiency and provision. Traditionally rendered "God Almighty." Genesis 17:1.

16
יְהוָה צִדְקֵנוּ
YAHWEH TSIDKENU — "yah-WEH tsid-KAY-noo"

"Tsedek" is righteousness or rightness; the suffix "-enu" makes it "our righteousness," not a righteousness earned. Jeremiah 23:6.

17
יְהוָה מְקַדִּשְׁכֶם
YAHWEH M'KADDESH — "yah-WEH meh-kah-DEESH"

From "qadosh," holy or set apart. The verb form here means the one doing the setting-apart. Exodus 31:13.

18
יְהוָה רֹעִי
YAHWEH ROHI — "yah-WEH ROH-ee"

"Ro'eh" is the word for shepherd, one who tends and leads flocks. Psalm 23:1.

19
יְהוָה יִרְאֶה
YAHWEH JIREH — "yah-WEH yir-EH"

From "ra'ah," to see, the same root behind El Roi, here in a form meaning "will see to it" or "will provide." Spoken at the exact place provision appeared. Genesis 22:14.

20
יְהוָה נִסִּי
YAHWEH NISSI — "yah-WEH NEE-see"

"Nes" is a banner or standard, raised after a battle to mark the victory, not raised as a plea before one. Exodus 17:15.

21
יְהוָה שָׁלוֹם
YAHWEH SHALOM — "yah-WEH shah-LOHM"

"Shalom" is broader than the English "peace," it carries wholeness and completeness, not just an absence of conflict. Judges 6:24.

22
אֵל גְּמֻלוֹת
EL GEMULOT — "el geh-moo-LOTE"

From "gamal," to deal with or repay, "gemulot" specifically means recompense, what is owed being repaid. Jeremiah 51:56.

23
יְהוָה רֹפְאֶךָ
YAHWEH RAPHA — "yah-WEH roh-FEH-kha"

From "rapha," to heal or mend, the same root behind the modern Hebrew word for doctor. Spoken right after bitter water turned sweet. Exodus 15:26.

24
גֹּאֵל
GO'EL — "go-EL"

The kinsman-redeemer, a specific legal role in ancient Israel, a family member with the right and duty to buy back what was lost or avenge what was wronged. Job 19:25.

25
עִמָּנוּ אֵל
IMMANU EL — "ee-mah-NOO el"

Built from "im" (with), "anu" (us), and "El" (God). Read left to right in transliteration but right to left in the Hebrew itself. Isaiah 7:14.
