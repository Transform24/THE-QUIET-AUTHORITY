The Bridge · Between The Armor And The Gates

Lord, Teach Us to Pray

The armor is not the end of the passage. Paul closes it with prayer, not a sword. Long before Paul wrote that, the disciples watched Jesus pray and asked him for the same thing you are being handed now. "Lord, teach us to pray..." Luke 11:1, King James Version

The Address

“Our Father which art in heaven, Hallowed be thy name.”

You do not open this prayer by asking for anything. You open it by naming who you are speaking to. Father, not a force. In heaven, not far off. Holy, not casual. Everything you are about to ask for gets weighed against who He already is.

The Surrender

“Thy kingdom come. Thy will be done in earth, as it is in heaven.”

This is the same posture the armor put you in. You put on truth and righteousness and peace so you could stand, and now you use that standing to ask for His will instead of your own. Surrender is not weakness here. It is the whole point of being armored in the first place.

The Dependence

“Give us this day our daily bread.”

Not this week's bread. Not this year's. Today's. You come back to this prayer tomorrow needing it again, and that repetition is not a failure to graduate past needing Him. It is the practice.

The Reckoning

“And forgive us our debts, as we forgive our debtors.”

You cannot ask to be forgiven with one hand and hold a grudge with the other. This line will not let you. It ties your own forgiveness to what you are willing to release, every single time you pray it.

The Deliverance

“And lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil: For thine is the kingdom, and the power, and the glory, for ever. Amen.”

The prayer ends where the armor began, standing against what wants to take you down, except now you are not standing alone. The kingdom, the power, and the glory were never yours to carry. They were always His.

The Whole Prayer

Our Father which art in heaven, Hallowed be thy name. Thy kingdom come, Thy will be done in earth, as it is in heaven. Give us this day our daily bread. And forgive us our debts, as we forgive our debtors. And lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil: For thine is the kingdom, and the power, and the glory, for ever. Amen.

Matthew 6:9-13, King James Version
